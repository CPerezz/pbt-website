Store the header as one record.

TL;DR Client databases read whole pages of 4 KB or more, and a full header with all 64 slots is about 2 KB. Keeping slots 0–63 in the account's own record makes of storage reads free on disk, for more per account read on average.

Aproposal & expectation

Clients answer most state reads from a flat database rather than from the tree. There, every storage slot is a record of its own, keyed by account and slot or kept in a separate storage table. So even a header slot costs a lookup of its own, although the account read just before it already found the header.

The proposal: store each account's header as one record, with its basic data, code hash or delegation, and its live slots 0–63. Reading an account then brings its hot slots along.

We expected it to be cheap, because databases never read single records: they read and cache whole pages or blocks. A full header with all 64 slots holds about 2 KB of values, within one page of any client's database.

Bdata
  • Each block reads accounts and distinct header slots. So of its storage reads would need no lookup of their own.
  • An account read would carry more on average. of account reads carry any header slots; in total about more per block.
  • Across all accounts, header slots hold of values ( slots). Today each of them also carries its own key of account and slot.

A full header record: 3 header values plus 64 slot values of 32 B, about 2.1 KB.

How we measured it: reads per block over the full sample, blocks in ranges of 1,000 (paths_reads). Record sizes come from 4 of 256 address prefixes over two days (c3_accessed, c3_live_header), which under-represents vanity addresses. Database units are the clients' default settings as of October 2026. Windows and checks: method.

Cconclusion

Supported. On reads, a header record stays well inside the page or block a client reads anyway, so header-slot reads become free on disk as well as in the tree. The cost is on writes: changing any header slot rewrites the whole record, more on average and about 2 KB at most, small next to the pages a database rewrites anyway. The header slots also stop needing keys of their own.

This is what makes the "free" in the header gas rule true for the disk. It needs no consensus change, so clients can adopt it on their own.
