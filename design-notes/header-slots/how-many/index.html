How many slots should the header hold?

TL;DR 4 slots, EIP-7864's number, catch only of the cold charges 64 do, and cost paths on writes. 32 and 64 are a few percent apart: 32 keep of the charges, and live headers would shrink by just . What 32 does halve is the most one header can hold, from 2 KB to 1 KB.

Aquestion & expectation

Where 4 comes from. EIP-8297 holds 64 slots. The number goes back to Vitalik's 2021 proposal for editable header fields and to EIP-6800 (Verkle), which kept 64 slots and 128 code chunks in the account stem. EIP-7864, the binary tree proposal, cut its header to 4 slots and 16 code chunks in June 2026 (ethereum/EIPs#11832) without stating a reason.

EIP-8369 gives one reason a small number can appeal. Its AA-VOPS profile has partial-state nodes keep the first few storage slots of every account, so they can validate account-abstraction transactions without a witness. Every slot kept costs each such node storage, so it considers 2 to 4, pending benchmarks.

Why 32. A 32-slot header halves the most one header can hold, from 64 slot values to 32 (2 KB to 1 KB). It shrinks nothing else: the reserved positions 3–63 and the unused 128–255 already cost nothing, since absent keys occupy no nodes. In live state, 32 only saves the slots 32–63 that contracts actually use.

The trade. Fewer header slots leave hot slots outside the header. They also move the header boundary lower, so more contracts write slots on both sides of it and need two paths where one would do. More header slots catch little extra, and make full headers heavier wherever they are stored or moved whole.

We expected 4 slots to be too few, since contracts spread their hot variables over more than their first four slots, and the gains to flatten well before 64. What a smaller header saves in size was the open part.

Cconclusion

4 is too few. It loses more than half of the cold charges 64 catch and adds paths on writes. In exchange it trims header values across all of mainnet from to , which is the saving a partial-state node would see.

32 or 64 stays open. The data puts them a few percent apart either way. 32 keeps of the cold charges and saves of live header data. The one thing 32 halves is the worst case, the most one header can hold. That matters only where a whole header is stored or moved as one unit, such as a one-record header in a client, and even there 2 KB fits within one database page. Trimming the reserved positions or 128–255 would save nothing, since absent keys cost nothing. On today's data the choice turns on whether the worst-case header size matters anywhere.
